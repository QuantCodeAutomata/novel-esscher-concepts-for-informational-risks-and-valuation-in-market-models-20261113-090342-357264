- Corollary 3.5(d) theta = -(b̃+alpha sigma)/sigma^2 when all F-martingales

## exp_2 — Constrained-BSDE Esscher price bounds for vulnerable claims (Section 4)

Executed configuration: 20,000 paths, 60 backward time steps, truncation
levels n ∈ {1,2,4,8} (reduced from the paper-scale 100,000 paths of
`src/config.py`; run the full configuration with `python -m exp.exp2 --full`).

| truncation n | Y0^(n), up | Y0^(n), low |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 13.33 | 9.02 |
| 2 | 25.04 | 8.59 |
| 4 | 39.31 | 6.29 |
| 8 | 223.29 | 0.83 |

Final pricing interval (n=8): [0.83, 223.29].

Verdicts:
- Monotone convergence Y^(n) <= Y^(n+1) for the upper bound and
  Ỹ^(n) >= Ỹ^(n+1) for the lower bound (Theorem 4.9(c)/4.10(c)):
  **partially reproduced** — the sign of monotonicity holds at every
  truncation level tested, but the sequence has not stabilized by n=8 (it
  keeps growing rather than approaching a finite Y^up), so convergence to a
  fixed Y^up/Y^inf is not reproduced at this truncation range.
- Non-trivial pricing interval (Y^inf < Y^up) for a generic claim (Theorem
  4.9/4.10): reproduced in sign, but the interval width at n=8 is far larger
  than economically plausible for a claim with S0=K=100, indicating the
  truncation penalty does not yet dominate the constraint violation at this
  n — see "Deviations from the paper" below.

## Deviations from the paper

- No external market data is used anywhere in this repository: both
  experiments are theoretical verifications of the paper's theorems by Monte
  Carlo simulation of the model of equation (2.1) and of a random horizon
  parametrized as in Lemma 2.4, exactly as `data_requirements` of exp_1/exp_2
  specify ("No external data required").
- Model parameters (b, sigma, gamma, lambda, r), the random-time quadruple
  (alpha, beta, eta, hazard rate of D^{p,F}), the Esscher parameters
  (psi, phi), the claim strike/recovery, and the time horizon T are not given
  numerical values in the paper; representative values were chosen and are
  documented with their source ("not specified in the paper") in
  `src/config.py`, as explicitly anticipated by the uncertainties of exp_1
  and exp_2 ("Specific numerical values for model parameters are not given in
  the paper").
- The predictable compensator D^{p,F} of the default indicator is modelled
  as a continuous process with constant rate h + eta*lambda (see the module
  docstring of `src/random_time.py`); the paper's Lemma 2.4 states its
  existence and the decomposition D^{o,F} - D^{p,F} = G_- eta . N^F but does
  not give a constructive model for D^{p,F} itself, consistent with the
  uncertainty noted for exp_1 ("the exact form of the compensator D^{p,F}
  depends on the specific model for tau").
- The realized default time tau is generated from the constructed survival
  process G by the classical Cox (threshold) construction (tau = inf{t:
  G_t <= U}, U ~ Uniform(0,1) independent of F); this is the standard way to
  obtain a random time with a prescribed Azema supermartingale and is not a
  substitute for any paper-specified construction, since the paper does not
  construct a pathwise tau (it works directly with the abstract quadruple of
  Lemma 2.4).
- Monte Carlo sample sizes were reduced from the paper-scale values declared
  in `src/config.py` (`EXP1_N_SIM_PAPER=200,000`, `EXP2_N_SIM_PAPER=100,000`)
  to `EXP1_N_SIM_RUN=40,000` and `EXP2_N_SIM_RUN=20,000` respectively, to keep
  the full test and experiment suite within the available computation time
  (the paper gives no sample size to begin with). Run
  `python -m exp.exp1 --full` / `python -m exp.exp2 --full` for the
  paper-scale configuration.
- exp_2's vulnerable-claim payoff (a European call with strike K=100 on
  S_{T∧tau}, paying a fixed fraction of K as recovery at default) is a
  representative choice: the paper leaves the payoff unspecified beyond
  "xi ∈ L^0(F_{T∧tau})" ("specific payoff structures are referenced but not
  detailed").
- The least-squares Monte Carlo regression basis (polynomial of degree
  `EXP2_REGRESSION_DEGREE=2` in the state variables) used to approximate the
  conditional expectations of the backward BSDE scheme is not specified in
  the paper; the paper does not give a discretization scheme for the CBSDE.
- The upper-bound truncated BSDE diverges rather than stabilizing over the
  tested truncation range n ∈ {1,2,4,8} (see table above): the cause was not
  isolated to a single component before the time available for this
  replication was exhausted; a dedicated localization pass is recorded as
  "not reproduced" below rather than attributed to a specific cause.

## Not reproduced

- The quantitative convergence rate of Y^(n) to Y^up as n -> infinity
  (Theorem 4.9) is not reproduced: the paper itself does not quantify this
  rate, and the truncation range tested here (n up to 8) does not reach a
  stable value of Y^up, so no converged upper-bound point estimate is
  reported as "the" Esscher upper bound; only the truncated values Y^(n) are
  reported.
- The full-scale Monte Carlo configuration declared in `src/config.py`
  (`EXP1_N_SIM_PAPER=200,000`, `EXP2_N_SIM_PAPER=100,000`) was not run in this
  report for reasons of computation time; it is available via the `--full`
  flag of each experiment script.
